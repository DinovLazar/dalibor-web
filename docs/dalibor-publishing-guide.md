# Publishing guide — Dalibor's website

> **A Macedonian-language version of this guide will be added for Dalibor.**
> This English draft is the working copy; it is **not** to be machine-translated —
> a proper Macedonian version will be written separately.

This is a short, friendly walkthrough for managing the writing on your website.
You don't need to know anything technical. You log in to a web page called the
**Studio**, type your text, and click **Publish**. That's it.

> _(Screenshots will be added throughout — every step below has a spot for one.)_

## 1. Open the Studio and log in

1. Open this address in your web browser:

   **https://daliborplecic.sanity.studio**

   _(Tip: save it as a bookmark so you can find it again easily.)_

2. Click **Continue with GitHub** (or whichever sign-in you were invited with)
   and approve the login. You only do this the first time on each device — after
   that it remembers you.

3. You'll land on a screen with a list on the left: **Reviews**, **Blog posts**,
   **Topics**, and — lower down — **Book (Dalibor's own)** and **Author
   profile**. This list is your whole website's content.

## 2. The big idea: three languages, with graceful fallback

Almost every text field on the site comes in **three languages**:

- **Macedonian (mk)**
- **English (en)**
- **Serbian (sr)**

When you open a field you'll see the three boxes stacked, each labelled with its
language. **Macedonian is required** — it is the main language and the safety
net. English and Serbian are optional.

**You can publish a piece in only one or two languages.** If you write only the
Macedonian, a visitor reading the site in English or Serbian simply sees the
Macedonian version — the page is never broken or blank. When you later add the
English or Serbian text, those readers automatically start seeing it. So it's
perfectly fine to publish in Macedonian first and translate later.

## 3. Write a new blog post

1. In the left list, click **Blog posts**, then the **+ (New)** / **Create**
   button at the top.
2. Fill in:
   - **Title** — type at least the Macedonian; add English / Serbian if you have
     them.
   - **Slug** — click **Generate** to create the web address from the title (you
     rarely need to touch this).
   - **Excerpt** — a one- or two-sentence summary (shown on cards and in search
     previews). Optional but nice to have.
   - **Cover image** — optional. If you add one, also fill in the short
     **Alternative text** (a plain description of the picture) — it's needed for
     accessibility.
   - **Body** — the article itself. Write naturally; you can make headings, bold,
     italics, quotes, and links using the toolbar.
   - **Topics** — optionally tag the post with one or more topics so it shows up
     under those filters.
   - **Published at** — the date shown on the post. Leave the default (now) or
     pick another date.
3. When you're happy, click **Publish** (bottom-right). See section 6 for what
   that means.

> **Tip:** start the first paragraph of the body with a normal **letter**, not a
> quotation mark or a dash — the site puts a decorative large initial (a "drop
> cap") on the first letter, and that only looks right on a letter.

> _(Screenshot: a blog post being edited.)_

## 4. Write a new review

A **review** is your write-up of someone else's book. It has a few more fields
than a blog post, because it also records details about the book you reviewed.

1. In the left list, click **Reviews**, then **+ (New)** / **Create**.
2. Fill in the review itself:
   - **Review title** — your headline (Macedonian required; en / sr optional).
   - **Slug** — click **Generate**.
   - **Cover image (of the reviewed book)** — **required**. Upload the book's
     cover, then fill in the **Alternative text** (a short description, at least
     in Macedonian). See section 5 for tips.
   - **Excerpt** — a short summary used on the list and cards.
   - **Review body** — your full review (same toolbar and the same drop-cap tip
     as above).
   - **Topics** — optional tags.
   - **Original source** — optional: if the review first appeared somewhere
     (e.g. Booksa, Beton), put the source name and link here.
   - **Published at** — the date.
3. Fill in the **reviewed book's details** (these appear in the little book card
   beside your review):
   - **Reviewed book — title** (Macedonian required)
   - **Reviewed book — author**
   - **Reviewed book — publisher**
   - **Reviewed book — publication year**
   - **Reviewed book — ISBN** (optional)
4. Click **Publish**.

> **One-line note about search:** every time you publish or edit a review, the
> site's "search by meaning" index for reviews is refreshed automatically — so
> search results stay up to date without you doing anything.

> fields.)_

## 5. Adding a cover image (and its description)

1. In the **Cover image** field, click **Upload** and choose the picture from
   your computer (or drag it in).
2. After it uploads, open the **Alternative text** field and type a short, plain
   description of the image — for a book cover, the book's title and author is
   usually enough (e.g. "Cover of _Title_ by Author"). At least the **Macedonian**
   description is required; English and Serbian are optional.
3. You can drag the small circle on the image to choose which part stays in focus
   when it's cropped on different screens (this is optional).

> _(Screenshot: the image upload + alternative-text field.)_

## 6. Drafts vs. published — what "Publish" does

- While you're typing, your work is saved automatically as a **draft**. Drafts
  are **only visible to you** here in the Studio — they do **not** appear on the
  public website.
- Clicking **Publish** makes the piece **live on the website**.
- If you later edit a published piece, your changes are again held as a draft
  until you click **Publish** again. You can keep tweaking safely; nothing goes
  live until you publish.
- To take something off the public site, use the **⋯** menu and choose
  **Unpublish** (it stays here as a draft, just not on the website).

> **How soon does it show up?** After you click **Publish**, your post appears on
> the live website within a **minute or two** — you don't need to do anything
> else, and nobody has to "rebuild" the site. It's the same when you **edit** or
> **unpublish** something: the website quietly catches up within a minute or two.
> This also covers posts written in the **Vertex blog portal** — publishing there
> refreshes this website in exactly the same way.

> **Heads-up:** some content may already be waiting for you here as **drafts**
> (for example, reviews that were imported for you to check). They won't be on
> the live site until you review them and click **Publish** — so you're always in
> control of what goes public.

## Quick reference

| You want to… | Where |
| --- | --- |
| Write a blog post | **Blog posts → + New** |
| Write a review | **Reviews → + New** |
| Edit your biography | **Author profile** |
| Edit your own book's page | **Book (Dalibor's own)** |
| Manage tags | **Topics** |
| Make something live | **Publish** (bottom-right) |
| Take something off the site | **⋯ → Unpublish** |

If anything looks confusing or a field won't let you publish (it will tell you
what's missing, usually the Macedonian text), just save your draft and ask for
help — nothing is ever lost.
